# Heat stress program

Cedar Valley Manufacturing · EHS-SOP-11 · Applies to production areas without climate control.

## When the program applies

The program is active when the heat index in a work area reaches 85°F, measured by the line lead at the start of the shift and after lunch.

## Water, rest, shade

- Cool drinking water within 200 feet of every workstation, enough for about one quart per worker per hour.
- One cooler serves no more than six workers.
- Rest breaks in a cooler area: 10 minutes every hour at a heat index of 90°F or more.

## Acclimatization

New and returning workers build up exposure over the first week, starting with about 20% of a normal shift in the heat.

## Recognizing heat illness

Dizziness, heavy sweating, cramps, nausea and confusion are reported at once. The worker is moved to a cool area and given water. Confusion or loss of consciousness is a medical emergency.

## Training

Line crews receive a heat stress toolbox talk before the warm season and again when a heat-related event occurs. Attendance is recorded on the sign-in sheet.

## Corrective action guidance

- Too few coolers for crew size: add coolers until the 1-per-6 ratio is met.
- No cool rest area: add shade or fans near the line.
- Heat-related illness: review the work/rest schedule for the area.
